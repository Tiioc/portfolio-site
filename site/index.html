InboxOwner

Self-initiated. Simulated data.

At the front desk, every department's email came to us to sort and forward, and nobody could tell who had answered what. InboxOwner reads a shared inbox's timestamps and senders, works out how long each kind of email actually waits, gives every email one owner with a reply target, and sorts new mail by topic, sending anything it isn't sure about to a person. On a made-up gym's inbox, built with slow cancellations planted in it to test the check, it showed cancellation requests waiting a median of 18.7 business hours while trial inquiries got answered in 1.2.

Order-to-Cash Tracker

Self-initiated. Simulated data.

I handled a lot of payment disputes at the front desk, and once I charged the wrong member because two people had the same name. This tracker follows an order from signed contract to collected cash, checks it against the customer's contract before anyone enters it, matches every payment to an invoice by customer ID, and drafts the escalation when something sticks. On a made-up company's orders, with mistakes planted to test it, it held 22 of 150 before entry and spotted that 41% of the "past due" money had already been paid under the wrong reference.

Front desk fixes I pitched at The Paseo Club

Real job. No code.

Situation. I worked the front desk at The Paseo Club, a members' club with tennis courts in Santa Clarita, CA, from December 2024 to December 2025. Every email for every department came to the desk first. We read it, figured out who it was for and forwarded it, and a fair amount of it got lost along the way and came back around to us. I also handled a lot of payment disputes myself. And on rainy days I was usually the first one in, so every call and email about whether the courts were open came to me, even though the desk didn't decide that.

Task. I wanted the desk to stop being the place where things got lost. Three things specifically: a clearer way to route email so it went to the right department the first time, fewer payment mistakes at the desk, and one place for closure decisions so the desk could give one answer on rainy days.

Action. I wrote up what I was seeing (email routing, payment mistakes, rainy-day closure calls) and took it to the head manager.

Result. That conversation is how I got moved into a Membership Advisor role, where I worked alongside the club's coordinator and saw how member setup, billing fixes, and department hand-offs actually run.
