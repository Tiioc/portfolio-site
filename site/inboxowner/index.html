A simple tool that helps a small team share one inbox without losing emails.

The problem

At The Paseo Club, every department's email came through the front desk. We forwarded it on, and nobody could tell who had answered what.

What I built

  • Measures how long each type of email waits for a reply.
  • Gives every email one owner, using a Gmail label.
  • Sorts new emails by topic, or sends them to a person.

What happened

18.7 hrs vs 1.2 hrs

Time to first reply: cancellation emails vs trial questions

87%

Sorted correctly on new emails

Tested on fake data made for it.

What I learned

My first sorting rules only worked on the examples they were built from, 98% on those and 22% on new emails. Testing also caught a bug where "lease" matched inside "please."

How I made it

I picked the problem, set the rules and tested everything. Claude Code wrote the code.

Try the demo Read the code
The details
  • The fake data. A made-up four-person gym with 589 email threads over 95 days. The reply times come from the last 90 days, which is 548 emails.
  • The 87%. I tested the final rules on 96 new emails written by a separate AI that never saw my rules. They sorted 81% on their own, and 87% of those went to the right topic. The rest went to a person.
  • The weak spot. Only half the cancellation emails got recognized on their own. I'd fix that before trusting it with anything that costs money.
  • How it's checked. 28 tests with hand-checked answers, and all 508 reply times worked out a second way to make sure they match.
  • Real inbox. There's a version built for a real Gmail inbox, but it hasn't been used by a real team yet. It never saves email text, only times, who replied and the topic.

Built in plain HTML and CSS with Claude Code, which I directed and fact-checked.
